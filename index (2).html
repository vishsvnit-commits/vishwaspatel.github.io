01 — About

From cuprite to charge transport.

Copper oxide exists in two everyday oxidation states — reddish Cu₂O (cuprite) and dark CuO (tenorite) — and the boundary between them is where most of my PhD lived. I work on turning low-cost, solution-processed metal oxides into high-bandgap, high-mobility hole transport layers (HTLs) for organic and perovskite solar cells, and on engineering the Ohmic-to-resistive switching behaviour of the same thin films for memristor and ReRAM applications.

My approach pairs hands-on thin-film fabrication — spin coating, electrodeposition, chemical precipitation — with device characterization (J–V, EQE, XRD, SEM, impedance spectroscopy) and numerical modelling in SCAPS-1D and the Transfer Matrix Method, so simulation and experiment inform each other rather than running in parallel.

I'm also increasingly interested in applying machine learning to predict solar cell output parameters (Voc, Jsc, FF, efficiency) directly from device and materials data.
